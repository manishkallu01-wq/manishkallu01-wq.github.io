This is the anchor of your portfolio. A hiring manager who reads only one page should read this one. Replace every bracketed placeholder, then delete this note.

The one-liner

We integrated [data sources] into [dataset] so that [stakeholder] can [decision they can now make].

Role: [e.g. I led the data pipeline and validation.] Team: [solo, or name + link teammates.] Repo: github.com/your_github_username/your-capstone-repo

The question

What problem were you solving, and why does it matter to a real stakeholder? Two or three sentences. No jargon a hiring manager would have to look up.

The data

Where the data came from, how much of it, and the one hard thing about it (missingness, joins across sources, scale, privacy). This is your data engineering story — the part the repo link backs up.

The approach

Your method at portfolio depth: shorter than your M4 write-up, longer than a tweet. What did you try, what did you keep, and why.

Findings

Lead with your best figure or your architecture diagram.

State the finding in plain language, then the caveat. Sparse and true beats full and shaky.
